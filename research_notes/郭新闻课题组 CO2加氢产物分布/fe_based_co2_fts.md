## Q1. Which Fe-based CO2 hydrogenation papers did the Guo group publish? (title, journal, year, DOI, authors)

### Takeaway
From abstracts alone, at least 15 Fe-based CO2-to-hydrocarbon papers with Xinwen Guo as co-author were identified, spanning 2014–2025. Most are with Chunshan Song (Penn State, later CUHK) and Anfeng Zhang, Min Liu, Fanshu Ding, Junhui Liu, Jie Zhu, Guanghui Zhang, Wenhui Li or Fei Yuan. The work falls into five lines:
- (a) FeK on Al2O3, with studies of support chemistry, coating and pore size (2014–2017);
- (b) MOF/ZIF-derived and N-doped-carbon-supported Fe (2016–2019);
- (c) Cu promotion and support effects (2018);
- (d) mechanistic work on Fe carbides: particle size, Fe5C2 role, oxidation vs carburization, support effects (2020–2022);
- (e) Fe–Co bimetallic systems: CoFe2O4, Na-CoFe2O4, LDH-derived, CoFe alloy carbides (2021–2025).

## Q2. Per-paper catalyst, conditions, conversion, CO selectivity and hydrocarbon distribution

### Takeaway
Only the headline numbers from abstracts could be retrieved. The full CH4 / C2=–C4= / C2–C4 paraffin / C5+ / CO slates and the carbon basis need the full texts. Where conditions are reported, they are typical CO2-FTS conditions: about 300–320 °C, 3 MPa, H2/CO2 = 3–4.

### Cited Findings

**(1) FeK/Al2O3, six commercial Al2O3 supports (IECR 2014)**
- **Catalyst:** FeK supported on six commercial Al2O3 supports, varying surface hydroxyl groups, SiO2 content and point of zero charge (PZC). The PZC controls Fe dispersion and particle size; a higher PZC gives finer Fe. — [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268152577_CO2_Hydrogenation_to_Hydrocarbons_over_Iron-based_Catalyst_Effects_of_Physicochemical_Properties_of_Al2O3_Supports)
- **Best result:** "The highest CO2 conversion (54.4%) and C5+ hydrocarbons selectivity (31.1%) were achieved when the PZC was 8.0." — [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268152577_CO2_Hydrogenation_to_Hydrocarbons_over_Iron-based_Catalyst_Effects_of_Physicochemical_Properties_of_Al2O3_Supports)
- **Missing:** conditions, CO selectivity and the other fractions.

**(2) SiO2-coated FeK/Al2O3 (RSC Adv 2014)**
- "The catalyst with 9 wt% SiO2 coating showed both high CO2 conversion (63%) and high selectivity toward C2+ hydrocarbons (74%), while further higher coating led to suppression of catalyst activity." — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260438124_Effect_of_SiO_2_-coating_of_FeKAl_2_O_3_catalysts_on_their_activity_and_selectivity_for_CO_2_hydrogenation_to_hydrocarbons)
- **Note:** 63% conversion is unusually high for CO2-FTS. The basis and conditions were not checked.

**(3) Fe on ZIF-8 and MIL-53(Al) supports (J CO2 Util 2016)**
- The MOFs stayed structurally intact at 3 MPa and 573 K (300 °C) with water present. CO2 conversion and light-olefin selectivity differed greatly between the two supports. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982016300154)
- A citing paper says these MOF supports outperformed γ-Al2O3 at 300 °C. — [ScienceDirect (MIL-53-derived Fe paper)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S221334372400191X)
- **Missing:** numerical selectivities.

**(4) Fe–K/Al2O3 with different support pore sizes (J CO2 Util 2017; Guo authorship unconfirmed)**
- **Catalysts:** 8 catalysts with Fe and K fixed at 15 and 10 wt%, made by incipient wetness impregnation. Larger pores gave larger Fe2O3 particles.
- **Result:** C2+ and C5+ selectivity follow a volcano trend, with the maximum at about 5–8 nm Fe2O3 (7–10 nm pores). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982016304760)
- **Missing:** numbers.

**(5) Fe-MOF (MIL-88B)-derived catalysts (J CO2 Util 2017)**
- **Preparation:** one-step pyrolysis of Fe-MIL-88B.
- **Phase effect:** catalysts containing some Fe3O4 plus χ-Fe5C2 performed better; metallic Fe and θ-Fe3C lowered CO2 conversion and hydrocarbon selectivity. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982017302366); summarized by search over [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318373629_Fe-MOF-derived_highly_active_catalysts_for_carbon_dioxide_hydrogenation_to_valuable_hydrocarbons)
- A review describes the two Fe-MOF-derived catalysts as having high stability and selectivity to light olefins and liquid fuels.
- **Missing:** numbers.
- **Caution:** do not attribute the "33.36% C2–C4= / 12.25% CH4" figures to this paper. They belong to the 2026 Na–K/Fe–Zn *Catal. Lett.* paper. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10562-026-05327-2)

**(6) ZIF-8-derived N-doped carbon with Fe/K (J CO2 Util 2018)**
- Adding Fe or Fe/K changed ZIF-8 pyrolysis, giving carbon-encapsulated Fe nanoparticles or metal-nanoparticle-in-hollow-sphere structures. FeZn-NC was stable for 126 h on stream. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018300106)
- **Missing:** selectivities.

**(7) Physical mixture of χ-Fe5C2 and K-modified Al2O3 (IECR 2018)**
- At H2/CO2 = 4: "CO2 conversion improved to 40.9%, and the C2+ selectivity to 73.5%, containing 37.3% C2–C4= and 31.1% C5+ value-added hydrocarbons." — search summary of [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/direct-transformation-of-carbon-dioxide-to-value-added-hydrocarbo/) / ACS abstract
- **Uncertain:** a second search could not re-find the 40.9% figure. It appeared in the first search summary as quoted abstract text, so treat it as likely but unverified.
- **Missing:** CO selectivity and CH4 (by difference, CH4 is at most about 26.5% of hydrocarbons if the 73.5% C2+ is on a hydrocarbon basis; this is an inference).

**(8) Cu-promoted Fe (ACS SCE 2018)**
- **Effect of Cu:** Cu doping lowers C2–C4= selectivity (secondary olefin hydrogenation) but raises C5+ (oligomerization). Cu increases CO2 adsorption, lowers CO selectivity, helps Fe reduction and acts as an RWGS site. Performance tracks the strength of the Fe–Cu interaction. — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/ascecg/article-abstract/6/8/10182/1318199/Selective-CO2-Hydrogenation-to-Hydrocarbons-on-Cu?redirectedFrom=fulltext); [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/selective-co-sub2sub-hydrogenation-to-hydrocarbons-on-cu-promoted/)
- A review describes related Liu et al. work using KFeCu on amorphous ZrO2 (KFeCu/a-ZrO2), and 3Fe–10Cu/MCM-41(T) as the best zeolite-supported variant. Whether these belong to the same paper or to a separate IECR 2018 "hybridization of supports" paper is **unclear**. — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11648294/)
- **Missing:** numbers.

**(9) ZnO + N-doped carbon overcoat on Fe3O4-based catalyst (IECR 2019)**
- Compared with the benchmark Fe3O4 catalyst, the reaction rate increased by about 25% and the olefin/paraffin (O/P) ratio rose from 0.07 to 1.68. — [Catalysts 2021 deactivation review](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/catal11121447)
- Reviews report improved activity and stability and high selectivity to light olefins and C5+. — [Frontiers Nanotechnol. review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nanotechnology/articles/10.3389/fnano.2022.978358/pdf)
- **Missing:** exact selectivities.

**(10) Particle size effect (ACS Catal 2020)**
- The apparent TOF rose 6–8-fold as the average Fe-carbide size fell from 7 to 2 nm, while CH4 and lower-olefin selectivity were unaffected. The size effect was separated into effects on primary reactions (CO2 activation/RWGS) and secondary reactions. — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.0c01526); search summary
- **Missing:** conditions and the selectivity slate.

**(11) Role of Fe5C2 (Catal Today 2021)**
- Reviews cite it for χ-Fe5C2 as the active phase for C2–C4 olefins. A related finding is that χ-Fe5C2 gives higher lower-olefin selectivity but weaker chain growth than θ-Fe3C. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343286835_Insight_into_the_Role_of_Fe5C2_in_CO2_Catalytic_Hydrogenation_to_Hydrocarbons)
- **Missing:** numbers.

**(12) CoFe2O4-derived Fe–Co (Catal Today 2021)**
- **Preparation:** CoFe2O4 made by a hydrothermal route.
- **Best catalyst:** Co1Fe2 (Co/Fe = 1/2). It reduces quickly to a CoxFey alloy, then carburizes to χ-(CoxFe1-x)5C2, the active phase for olefins. Close Fe–Co contact suppresses CH4 and favors C2+, especially lower olefins. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920586120305484)
- **Missing:** numbers.

**(13) Na-CoFe2O4 (follow-up)**
- "C2–C4= space time yield as high as 2.88 μmol(C2–C4=) gcat⁻¹ s⁻¹ and olefin to paraffin ratio about 6 at CO2 conversion higher than 41%." No significant deactivation over 100 h. Na promotes chain growth and suppresses direct hydrogenation of Fe-(CH2)n intermediates. — [ivySCI abstract](https://www.ivysci.com/en/articles/383626__Boosting_light_olefin_selectivity_in_CO2_hydrogenation_by_adding_Co_to_Fe_catalysts_within_close_pro)
- Another paper cites this as 41.8% CO2 conversion. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1004954125004653)

**(14) Iron catalysts under oxidation vs carburization (Sci Adv 2022)**
- **Mechanism:** Fe(0) carburizes to θ-Fe3C and then χ-Fe5C2. By-product H2O re-oxidizes the carbide to Fe3O4, which drives deactivation. Tuning H2O/H2 controls the balance between carburization and oxidation. — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abm3629)
- **Selectivity snippet:** "highest C2+ selectivity of 50% (CO2 conversion of ~40%) at the steady state is achieved at an optimized H/C ratio of 5." This is a snippet, not verified in the full text. — [Science / PMC8816344](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8816344/)

**(15) Effect of support properties on supported Fe (Fuel c. 2022; Guo unconfirmed)**
- ZrO2 supports favor olefin selectivity and chain growth. Spent FeNa/ZrO2 has the most Fe5C2. A higher surface C/H ratio inhibits olefin hydrogenation. The support sets the final Fe3O4/FexCy ratio and the carbide composition. Na and Mn promoters were also studied. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016236122014971)
- **Missing:** numbers.

**(16) In-promoted FeK/Al2O3 (J CO2 Util 2022)**
- CO2 conversion over 10Fe2In1K/Al2O3 is 17% higher than over 10Fe1K/Al2O3. CO selectivity falls slightly and the hydrocarbon distribution "changed a little." In speeds up the reduction of Fe oxide. — [SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4189393); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982022003626)
- **Conflict:** another snippet says In *decreased* reducibility. Unresolved.

**(17) Fe–Co–Mg LDH-derived catalysts (IECR 2023)**
- **Preparation:** Fe–Co–Mg LDH precursors with different Fe/Co ratios, compared with impregnated Fe–Co/MgO.
- **Best catalyst:** FeCo-9:1-LDH gave "C2–C4= over 36% at a CO2 conversion higher than 40%." It carburizes to a Co-modified Fe carbide. — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/iecred/article-abstract/62/21/8210/1104920/Boosting-the-Production-of-Light-Olefins-from-CO2?redirectedFrom=fulltext)

**(18) CoFe alloy carbides (ACS Catal 2025)**
- Tuning the carburization degree and phase composition of the CoFe alloy carbides raises the C2+ olefin STY to 328.1 mg gcat⁻¹ h⁻¹, for a catalyst pre-carburized in H2/CO = 2 at 320 °C. Optimizing the synergy between the two components improves CO2 activation and C–C coupling. — [CUHK portal](https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/fine-tuning-the-active-phases-of-cofe-alloy-carbides-for-boosting/)

### Inferences
- Where numbers exist, the group's typical performance is 40–55% CO2 conversion with about 30–37% C2–C4= or about 31% C5+. Oxygenates are not reported in any abstract found.
- In item (7), 37.3% C2–C4= plus 31.1% C5+ gives 68.4% out of 73.5% C2+, which implies about 5.1% C2–C4 paraffins. CH4 would then be about 26.5% if the basis is hydrocarbons only. This is arithmetic on the abstract numbers, not a reported value.

### Gaps
- No paper could be checked for the CO-free vs CO-inclusive basis, GHSV, or time on stream (apart from the 126 h, 100 h and steady-state mentions).
- Full product slates (CH4 and C2–C4 paraffins in particular) are missing for every paper. They need publisher full text or the group's 2022 *Chin. J. Catal.* review tables, which could not be fetched.

## Q3. Best reported numbers and mechanistic claims; jet-fuel/SAF flag

### Takeaway
Among the abstract-level numbers retrieved:
- **Highest light-olefin selectivity:** 37.3% C2–C4= at 40.9% conversion (Fe5C2 + K-Al2O3, 2018), followed by more than 36% at more than 40% conversion (FeCo-9:1-LDH, 2023).
- **Best olefin productivity:** 2.88 μmol g⁻¹ s⁻¹ C2–C4= STY with O/P ≈ 6 (Na-CoFe2O4), and 328.1 mg g⁻¹ h⁻¹ C2+ olefins (CoFe alloy carbide, 2025).
- **Highest C5+:** 31.1%, reported in both the 2014 Al2O3-PZC paper and the 2018 Fe5C2 paper.
- **Jet fuel / SAF:** no Guo-authored CO2-to-jet-fuel (C8–C16) or SAF paper was found.

The group's consistent mechanistic picture is a tandem RWGS-on-Fe3O4 plus FTS-on-χ-Fe5C2 scheme. They emphasize carbide protection against H2O oxidation, particle size (TOF), promoters (K/Na for chain growth and olefin retention; Cu for RWGS and oligomerization; Zn/N-C overcoats; In for reduction) and Fe–Co proximity forming χ-(CoxFe1-x)5C2.

### Cited Findings

**Best numbers (as stated in abstracts):**
- 37.3% C2–C4=, 31.1% C5+, 73.5% C2+ at 40.9% CO2 conversion, H2/CO2 = 4 (Fe5C2 + K-Al2O3). — [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/direct-transformation-of-carbon-dioxide-to-value-added-hydrocarbo/)
- More than 36% C2–C4= at more than 40% CO2 conversion (FeCo-9:1-LDH). — [ACS IECR 2023](https://pubs.acs.org/iecred/article-abstract/62/21/8210/1104920/Boosting-the-Production-of-Light-Olefins-from-CO2?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- C2–C4= STY of 2.88 μmol g⁻¹ s⁻¹, O/P ≈ 6, more than 41% conversion, 100 h stable (Na-CoFe2O4). — [ivySCI](https://www.ivysci.com/en/articles/383626__Boosting_light_olefin_selectivity_in_CO2_hydrogenation_by_adding_Co_to_Fe_catalysts_within_close_pro)
- C2+ olefin STY of 328.1 mg gcat⁻¹ h⁻¹ (CoFe alloy carbides). — [CUHK portal](https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/fine-tuning-the-active-phases-of-cofe-alloy-carbides-for-boosting/)
- 54.4% CO2 conversion with 31.1% C5+ (FeK/Al2O3, PZC 8.0). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268152577_CO2_Hydrogenation_to_Hydrocarbons_over_Iron-based_Catalyst_Effects_of_Physicochemical_Properties_of_Al2O3_Supports)
- 63% CO2 conversion with 74% C2+ (9 wt% SiO2-coated FeK/Al2O3). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260438124_Effect_of_SiO_2_-coating_of_FeKAl_2_O_3_catalysts_on_their_activity_and_selectivity_for_CO_2_hydrogenation_to_hydrocarbons)
- O/P raised from 0.07 to 1.68 by the ZnO/N-C overcoat. — [Catalysts review](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/catal11121447)

**Mechanistic claims:**
- **Active phases:** χ-Fe5C2 plus some Fe3O4 are beneficial; metallic Fe and θ-Fe3C are detrimental (Fe-MOF 2017). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982017302366)
- **Carburization path:** Fe → θ-Fe3C → χ-Fe5C2. H2O oxidizes the carbide to Fe3O4 and causes deactivation; protecting the carbide is key; H2O/H2 is a control knob. — [Science Advances 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abm3629)
- **Size effect:** a 6–8× TOF gain from 7 to 2 nm carbide, with CH4 and olefin selectivity size-insensitive. — [ACS Catal 2020](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.0c01526)
- **Cu promotion:** RWGS site and better Fe reduction; drives olefin hydrogenation (lower C2–C4=) and oligomerization (higher C5+). — [ACS SCE 2018](https://pubs.acs.org/ascecg/article-abstract/6/8/10182/1318199/Selective-CO2-Hydrogenation-to-Hydrocarbons-on-Cu?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- **Fe–Co:** close contact forms χ-(CoxFe1-x)5C2, suppresses CH4 and favors lower olefins. Na adds chain growth and suppresses direct hydrogenation of Fe-(CH2)n. — [ScienceDirect Catal Today](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920586120305484); [ivySCI](https://www.ivysci.com/en/articles/383626__Boosting_light_olefin_selectivity_in_CO2_hydrogenation_by_adding_Co_to_Fe_catalysts_within_close_pro)
- **Supports:** ZrO2 gives more Fe5C2 and a higher surface C/H, which inhibits olefin hydrogenation (Guo authorship unconfirmed). Al2O3 PZC controls Fe dispersion. — [ScienceDirect Fuel](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016236122014971); [ResearchGate IECR 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268152577_CO2_Hydrogenation_to_Hydrocarbons_over_Iron-based_Catalyst_Effects_of_Physicochemical_Properties_of_Al2O3_Supports)
- **Tandem framework:** RWGS over Fe3O4 followed by FTS over Fe carbides. This is the general framework described alongside the group's work, not a claim specific to the group. — [ScienceDirect Appl Catal A (Xu et al., not Guo)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926860X22002058)

**Jet-fuel / SAF flag:**
- A targeted search found **no Xinwen Guo-authored CO2-to-jet-fuel (C8–C16) or SAF paper**. The jet-fuel papers that did appear belong to other groups:

  - Na-CoFe alloy: 63.5% C8–C16 at 10.2% conversion, 240 °C, 3 MPa (*The Innovation* 2021). — [The Innovation](https://www.cell.com/the-innovation/fulltext/S2666-6758(21)00095-3)
  - CO2-FTS coupled with toluene alkylation: C8–C16 up to 75% (*ACS Catal.* 2025, by Jiang, Jin, Wei ... Chenguang Wang, CAS GIEC; **not Guo**). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c02897)
- The group's own 2022 *Chin. J. Catal.* review discusses value-added hydrocarbons including liquid fuels. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358716705_Recent_advances_in_application_of_iron-based_catalysts_for_CO_hydrogenation_to_value-added_hydrocarbons)

### Inferences
- The Guo group's main contributions to Fe-based CO2 FTS are mechanistic: carbide dynamics, size effects and promoter roles, published in *Science Advances* and *ACS Catalysis*. Their record selectivities (about 36–37% C2–C4=) are moderate compared with the best Fe-based CO2-to-olefin reports from other groups (for example, 66.1% high-value olefins in the DICP Ba/Na work).
- Guo's group pursues CO2-to-aromatics and jet-range products mainly through oxide/zeolite (OX-ZEO) tandem routes rather than Fe-FTS. That is outside this file's scope and is covered in the sibling note on bifunctional zeolite routes.
