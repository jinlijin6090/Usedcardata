## Which papers did the group publish on bifunctional/tandem CO2 hydrogenation? (title, journal, year, DOI, authors)

### Takeaway
I could confirm five Guo-authored papers on one-step CO2 hydrogenation. Four use a zeolite:
1. ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5 to aromatics (J. CO2 Util. 2019; Guo's authorship there is only probable).
2. Co–In2O3/ZrO2 + nanosheet HZSM-5 to gasoline (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 2022).
3. Fe–Co + HZSM-5 nanosheets to gasoline (Fuel 2024).
4. GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5 to gasoline (Adv. Sci. 2026).

The fifth, CoFe alloy carbides to olefins (ACS Catal. 2025), uses no zeolite. A GaZrOx–ZSM-5 aromatics paper (ACS Catal. 2025) is probably theirs. Several 2026 GaZrOx / ZnZrOx dual-zeolite and p-xylene papers are possibly theirs but unconfirmed. Many well-known oxide/zeolite papers that search engines credit to Guo actually belong to other groups (see the "NOT GUO" list).

## Per-paper conditions, conversion, CO selectivity and hydrocarbon distribution

### Takeaway
Only headline numbers are available. Missing for every paper: the full CH4 / C2–C4= / C2–C4° / C5+ / aromatics-breakdown / oxygenate split, mass ratios, mixing mode, GHSV and H2/CO2. The available numbers suggest that hydrocarbon selectivities are reported CO-free:
- ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5 (2019): about 70% aromatics in hydrocarbons, against about 40% on a CO2-carbon basis per a citing paper.
- GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5 (2026): 83.2% C5+ alongside a separately reported 11.7% CO selectivity.

### Cited Findings

**1. ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5, aromatics (J. CO2 Util. 2019)**
- At 613 K (340 °C), aromatics selectivity *in hydrocarbons* reached 70% at 9% CO2 conversion, with CH4 selectivity below 1% (abstract, as quoted in search results). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)
- "A small amount of strong acid sites on ZSM-5 is indispensable for highly selective aromatics production." [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)
- The authors demonstrated a CO2 → CH3OH → aromatics pathway. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)
- A later citing paper (CCS Chem., H-[Fe]-ZSM-5/ZnO-ZrO2) restates this work as 9.1% CO2 conversion with 40.2% "CO2 to aromatics" selectivity. That figure is on a basis that likely *includes* CO, so it is not directly comparable to the 70% hydrocarbon-basis figure. This is my inference about the basis. [CCS Chemistry](https://www.chinesechemsoc.org/doi/10.31635/ccschem.024.202404893)
- Not found: pressure, H2/CO2, GHSV, oxide:zeolite mass ratio, mixing mode, CO selectivity, time on stream, BTX / C9+ split.

**2. Co–In2O3/ZrO2 + nanosheet HZSM-5, gasoline (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 2022)**
- HZSM-5 nanosheets with b-axis thickness from 2 nm to 2 μm were synthesised and composited with (Co-promoted) In2O3/ZrO2. [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00460)
- The 20 nm sample gave the **highest C5+ selectivity at matched (isoconversional) conditions** and showed **no deactivation after 150 h**. It also had less coke than the 2 μm sample. [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00460)
- Not found: numerical CO2 conversion, C5+ selectivity, CO selectivity, isoparaffin or aromatics fractions, conditions.

**3. Fe–Co + HZSM-5 nanosheets, gasoline (Fuel 2024)**
- The best catalyst, Fe-Co/HZ5-50-30, gave a **maximum C5+ selectivity of about 62.7% at 36.3% CO2 conversion**. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016236123025188); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Fuel..35729904Z/abstract)
- Division of labour: Fe–Co converts CO2 to light olefins via RWGS–FTS, and the HZSM-5 nanosheets upgrade the olefins to C5+.
- The ultrathin nanosheets "with suitable acidity" raise the liquid-hydrocarbon yield and reduce deactivation. [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Fuel..35729904Z/abstract)
- Not found: CO selectivity, CH4, isoparaffin/aromatics split inside C5+, conditions, TOS.

**4. GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5, gasoline (Adv. Sci. 2026)**
- Best catalyst: GZO-700/Ga-Z5(50).
- At **320 °C and 3 MPa**: **CO2 conversion 17.2%**, **C5+ selectivity 83.2%**, **CH4 below 1%**, **CO selectivity 11.7%**.
- **No significant deactivation over 400 h** on stream.
- Sources: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/); [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/advs.76074)
- Not found: H2/CO2, GHSV, mass ratio, integration mode, isoparaffin vs. aromatics split.

**5. GaZrOx–ZSM-5, aromatics (ACS Catal. 2025; PROBABLE Guo)**

- Source: [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c01780)
- Not found: conditions, CO selectivity, BTX / C9+ split.

**6. GaZrOx + dual zeolite, light aromatics (CEJ 2026; UNCONFIRMED attribution)**

**7. Dual-zeolite relay, ZnZr oxide + SAPO-34 + ZSM-5 (2026; UNCONFIRMED attribution)**

**8. Fe-ZnGa2O4/SAPO-34, light olefins (Appl. Catal. B 2024; UNCONFIRMED attribution)**

### Inferences
- In the Adv. Sci. 2026 paper, the 83.2% C5+ is reported separately from the 11.7% CO selectivity. The C5+ figure is therefore almost certainly a CO-free hydrocarbon basis. Under that assumption the C5+ yield would be about 17.2 × (1 − 0.117) × 0.832 ≈ 12.6%. This is my calculation, not a reported number.
- For the 2019 paper, the 70% figure is explicitly "in hydrocarbons" (CO-free). The roughly 40% figure quoted by others appears to be on an all-carbon basis that includes CO, which suggests CO selectivity was large (roughly 40–45%). This is an inference only.

## Best reported numbers and mechanistic claims

### Takeaway
Best confirmed numbers:
- **Gasoline:** 83.2% C5+ at 17.2% CO2 conversion, CO 11.7%, CH4 below 1%, stable more than 400 h (GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5, 2026).
- **Aromatics:** 82.8% at 14.3% conversion (GaZrOx–ZSM-5, 2025, probable Guo), and 70% in hydrocarbons at 9% conversion (ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5, 2019).
- **Highest conversion (Fe route):** 36.3% CO2 conversion with about 62.7% C5+ (Fe–Co/HZSM-5 nanosheets, 2024).

Mechanistic claims by route:
- **Methanol-mediated (oxide) route:** ZnZrOx-type oxides.
- **Olefin-mediated FT route:** Fe-based catalysts.
- **Zeolite side:** strong but limited Brønsted acidity, thin nanosheets for diffusion and coke resistance, and Ga substitution to tune acidity.
- **Oxide side:** Ga–H / HCOO* species and oxygen vacancies.

### Cited Findings
- **Methanol route.** CO2 → CH3OH → aromatics over ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5. A small amount of strong acid sites on ZSM-5 is indispensable for high aromatics selectivity. [ScienceDirect, J. CO2 Util. 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)
- **Zeolite morphology/diffusion.** 20 nm nanosheet HZSM-5 gives the highest C5+ selectivity at isoconversion and resists coking over 150 h. Co promotes H spillover from metallic Co onto ZrO2, which facilitates formation and hydrogenation of the key intermediate. [ACS, IECR 2022](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00460)
- **Olefin-mediated route (Fe).** Fe–Co makes light olefins, and HZSM-5 nanosheets with suitable acidity oligomerise and upgrade them to C5+, improving liquid yield and stability. [ADS, Fuel 2024](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Fuel..35729904Z/abstract)
- **Ga-based oxide and zeolite.**
  - Raising the GaZrOx calcination temperature (700 °C optimal) balances Ga–H coverage against O-vacancy concentration, which improves H2 dissociation, H spillover/migration and CO2 activation.
  - Substituting Ga for Al in ZSM-5 changes the acidity, which improves gasoline selectivity and slows deactivation.
  - [PubMed, Adv. Sci. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/)
- **Ga–H / HCOO*.** High Ga–H coverage on GaZrOx facilitates HCOO* intermediates. Homolytic H2 dissociation, triggered by an initial heterolytic step, is reported as the dominant mechanism. [ACS Catal. 2025](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c01780)
- **Silicalite-1 for p-xylene (UNCONFIRMED attribution).**
  - The strong Brønsted acid sites of conventional ZSM-5 cause uncontrolled (de)alkylation and xylene isomerisation.
  - Remedy: ZnZrOx is deposited by deposition–precipitation onto silicalite-1-coated AS-1, which shortens the methanol transfer distance during CO2 hydrogenation coupled with toluene alkylation, targeting para-xylene.
  - [Springer FCSE 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11705-026-2674-2)

### Inferences
- Across the confirmed papers, the group's main levers are on the zeolite side: thickness, acidity and Ga substitution. Methane is suppressed to below 1% in the oxide-based (methanol-route) systems. The Fe-based route gives much higher CO2 conversion (36%) but lower C5+ selectivity than the oxide route (17% conversion, 83% C5+). This is the usual activity–selectivity trade-off between the FT route and the methanol route.
- None of the results seen describe proximity or integration-mode studies (dual-bed vs. granule vs. powder mixing) by the Guo group.

- Not found: light-olefin results (SAPO-34/SSZ-13) from a confirmed Guo paper.
