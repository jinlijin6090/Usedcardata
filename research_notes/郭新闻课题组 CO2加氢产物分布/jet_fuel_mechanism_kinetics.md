## (A) Has the Guo Xinwen group published CO2 → jet fuel (C8–C16 / 航空煤油)? Catalysts, conditions, product distribution; closest group work

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed paper, conference abstract or patent from the Guo group on CO2 → jet fuel / C8–C16 / aviation kerosene**. The evidence of jet-fuel work is limited to:
- a 科技日报 / DUT news profile saying the team "is tackling CO2 hydrogenation to green aviation kerosene", with no performance figures;
- an industry project funded by 恒力石化 (大连) titled only "二氧化碳加氢制化学品" (2024-07 to 2025-06).

The group's closest published work is in gasoline and aromatics:
- **GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5, gasoline (Adv. Sci. 2026):** 17.2% CO2 conversion, 83.2% C5+, CO 11.7%, CH4 below 1%, at 320 °C and 3 MPa.

**Mechanistic levers the group reports**
- The GaZrOx calcination temperature (700 °C optimal) balances Ga–H coverage against oxygen-vacancy concentration. This aids H2 dissociation, H spillover and CO2 activation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/)
- Gallium-directed zeolite construction allows precise tuning of acidity, which maximises gasoline-range selectivity. [PMC13336408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13336408/)
- In the 2025 aromatics paper, high Ga–H coverage favours HCOO* (formate) intermediates. [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c01780)

### Inferences
- The group's 2026 GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5 platform reports 83.2% C5+, but MFI (ZSM-5) is a 10-membered-ring zeolite. Products from MFI-based OX-ZEO are usually concentrated in about C5–C11, so the share above C11 is likely small. This is inferred; no carbon-number distribution was retrieved.
- A Guo jet-fuel catalyst would most plausibly come from three changes to that platform. This is speculation from the group's stated aim and toolset, not reported work:
  - a larger-pore or 1D zeolite;
  - lower acid density, to reduce β-scission;
  - a downstream oligomerisation or alkylation step.
- **Approximate C5+ yield for the 2026 paper:** 17.2 × (1 − 0.117) × 0.832 ≈ 12.6% of the CO2 fed. This is my calculation and assumes C5+ is reported on a CO-free hydrocarbon basis.

## (A') Other groups' CO2 → jet-fuel (C8–C16) results, 2018–2026, for benchmarking (all NOT GUO)

### Takeaway
Almost all jet-range CO2 results come from **Fe- or CoFe-based modified-FTS catalysts**, not from methanol-mediated oxide/zeolite catalysts:
- Fe–Mn–K: 47.8% C8–C16 at 38.2% conversion.
- Na–CoFe alloy: 63.5% C8–C16 at 10.2% conversion.
- FTS coupled with toluene alkylation: up to 75% C8–C16.
- Fe + Pt/HZSM-22 hydroisomerisation targeting C8–C16 iso-paraffins.

The only oxide/zeolite route explicitly aimed at aviation fuel is ZnZrOx/Hβ CO2 hydrogenation coupled with **naphthalene alkylation**, giving 86.4% alkylnaphthalene selectivity for high-density fuel. Pure oxide/zeolite (OX-ZEO) CO2 systems typically give 70–80% C5+ (gasoline), with CO2 conversion below 20% and CO selectivity above 30%. Chinese pilot-scale "CO2-to-jet" claims (Shanghai prototype; XJTU appraisal) report more than 70–80% jet / C8+ selectivity, but these are not peer-reviewed.

### Cited Findings
**Fe and CoFe catalysts (FTS-type, CO-mediated)**
- **Fe–Mn–K (organic combustion synthesis), Nat. Commun. 2020** (Oxford group):
  - CO2 conversion 38.2%; C8–C16 selectivity 47.8%; CO 5.6%; CH4 10.4%.
  - Fe–Mn–Na gives 44.4% and Fe–Mn–Cs 44.0% C8–C16.
  - DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-20214-z. [Nature Commun.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20214-z)
- **Na-modified CoFe alloy (from LDH precursors), The Innovation 2021:**
  - At 240 °C and 3 MPa: C8–C16 selectivity 63.5% at 10.2% CO2 conversion, with CO + CH4 below 22%.
  - The authors note that ASF statistics cap C8–C16 at about 41% for a conventional FT route.
  - [The Innovation](https://www.cell.com/the-innovation/fulltext/S2666-6758(21)00095-3); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666675821000953)
- **Fe-based FTS + Pt/HZSM-5 or Pt/HZSM-22 tandem, Chem. Eng. J. 2025:**

  - [ScienceDirect S1385894725078362](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1385894725078362); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396065566_Selective_production_of_jet-range_hydrocarbons_via_CO2_hydrogenation_over_Fe-based_and_Ptzeolite_tandem_catalysts)

### Inferences
- For a benchmark C8–C16 "target", the peer-reviewed state of the art is about **48–75% C8–C16 within hydrocarbons**, and every such result is from Fe or CoFe chemistry, sometimes with alkylation or hydroisomerisation. No oxide/zeolite (methanol-route) catalyst in the retrieved literature reports a C8–C16 alkane selectivity.
- A Guo-type OX-ZEO jet catalyst would probably need either a second-stage oligomerisation or alkylation step, or a 1D/large-pore zeolite. The Pt/HZSM-22 example and the Hβ alkylation route show both strategies are used by others.
- The ASF ceiling of about 41% C8–C16 stated by the CoFe authors is the key argument for why tandem (zeolite-shaped) distributions are needed for jet fuel.

- Not seen: conditions (H2/CO2, GHSV) and PIONA splits for the Fe-based jet papers.
- The Shanghai prototype's catalyst type is not disclosed in the snippets.

## (B) Mechanism of oxide/zeolite tandem CO2 → hydrocarbons: intermediates, oxide step, zeolite step, topology/acidity effects, CH4 and CO formation

### Takeaway
For In2O3-, ZnZrOx- and GaZrOx-type OX-ZEO catalysts, the evidence favours a **methanol/DME-mediated route**:
- **Oxide step:** CO2 → HCOO* → CH3O* → CH3OH on oxygen-vacancy sites (In2O3) or on Ga–H / Zn–H sites.
- **Zeolite step:** methanol/DME enters the zeolite hydrocarbon-pool (dual-cycle) chemistry.

Supporting evidence:
- methanol and DME are seen by solid-state NMR in the used ZSM-5;
- DFT on ZSM-5 favours CO insertion into surface methoxy over ketene chemistry;
- for ZnCrOx|SAPO-34 (syngas, used here by analogy), methanol is the transport intermediate, while ketene may still dominate the route to ethene.

The other key points:
- **CO** comes from RWGS on the oxide, which competes with methanol synthesis. Its apparent Ea (about 117 kJ/mol) is higher than that of methanol formation (about 103 kJ/mol), so higher T favours CO.
- Draining methanol into the zeolite pulls methanol synthesis past its thermodynamic limit and suppresses RWGS, but only if the components are in close proximity and do not undergo damaging ion exchange.
- **CH4** is very low (about 1%) because the oxide does not form surface carbides. Zeolite-side hydrogenation of methyl species and cracking are the main CH4/C1 sources.

### Cited Findings
**Methanol-mediated versus ketene-mediated route**
- **Nat. Commun. 2021, "Selectivity descriptors for the direct hydrogenation of CO2 to hydrocarbons during zeolite-mediated bifunctional catalysis":**
  - Methanol and DME are detected in post-reaction ZSM-5 by solid-state NMR.
  - The authors cite DFT showing that CO insertion into surface methoxy on ZSM-5 is more exothermic than ketene conversion.
  - The paper frames selectivity through zeolite descriptors (topology, acidity).
  - DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-26090-5. [Nature Commun.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26090-5); [PMC8501036](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8501036/)
- **Tandem In2O3/HZSM-5 (ACS Sustain. Chem. Eng. 2024):**
  - Proposes methanol synthesis on In2O3 followed by MTH, with **hydrogenation of methyl species as the likely kinetically relevant step**.
  - **Intermediate transfer rates** (proximity) and **solid-state ion exchange** (In migrating onto Brønsted sites) decide whether the two components act bifunctionally.
  - DOI 10.1021/acssuschemeng.3c08250. [PMC10988559](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10988559/)
- **ZnCrOx|SAPO-34 (syngas-to-olefins; analogy only):** concludes that **methanol is the primary transport intermediate between oxide and zeolite, while ketene is the dominant intermediate for ethene**. It also notes that ketene has been proposed for OX-ZEO, DME carbonylation, MTH and CO2 hydrogenation, but its zeolite pathway "remains unclear". [ScienceDirect S204165202500570X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S204165202500570X)
- **On FT-type (Fe) surfaces:** ketene formed from surface CO and CH2 has been proposed to block chain growth and break the ASF distribution. This is a different mechanism from OX-ZEO. [Nat. Commun. 2019 perspective](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-13638-9)
- **Methanol mediation in Guo papers:** the group demonstrated a CO2 → CH3OH → aromatics pathway over ZnO/ZrO2–ZSM-5. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)

**Oxide step: formate/methoxy pathway, oxygen vacancies, RWGS competition**
- **Pathway.** DFT on In2O3(110) shows methanol forms mainly via the HCOO (formate) pathway at **oxygen-vacancy** sites. [ResearchGate, "Active Oxygen Vacancy Site for Methanol Synthesis from CO2 Hydrogenation on In2O3(110): A DFT Study"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236677737_Active_Oxygen_Vacancy_Site_for_Methanol_Synthesis_from_CO2_Hydrogenation_on_In2O3110_A_DFT_Study)
- **Microkinetics on In2O3.** First-principles microkinetic studies exist:
  - CO2 → CH3OH over In2O3(110), ACS Catal. 2021 (Chalmers). [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/accacs/article/11/15/9996/379007/A-First-Principles-Based-Microkinetic-Study-of-CO2)
  - Mechanism and microkinetics of methanol synthesis on indium oxide, J. Catal. 2018. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021951718301143)
- **Vacancies alone are not the descriptor.** One kinetic study notes that activity **cannot be simply correlated with surface oxygen-vacancy abundance**. [ACS Catal. 2021, 10.1021/acscatal.0c05046](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.0c05046)
- **Apparent activation energies on In2O3 (search-summary figures; exact source paper uncertain):**
  - Experimental: **103 kJ/mol for CO2 → methanol** versus **117 kJ/mol for RWGS**.
  - DFT values quoted in a review: **90 and 110 kJ/mol** for the same two reactions.
  - [In2O3 review PMC10095753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10095753/); [J. Catal. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021951718301143)
- **GaZrOx sites.** Ga–H drives HCOO* formation. Homolytic H2 dissociation, triggered by an initial heterolytic step, is reported as dominant. [ACS Catal. 2025](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c01780)
- **CO2 conversion ceiling.** OX-ZEO CO2 conversion is typically below 20% and CO selectivity above 30%. This reflects the methanol thermodynamic limit at the roughly 300–400 °C needed by the zeolite, together with RWGS competition. [Catal. Today review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920586123002432)

**Zeolite step: topology and acidity effects**
- **Strong acid sites.** A small amount of strong acid sites on ZSM-5 is indispensable for high aromatics selectivity (hydrogen transfer and aromatisation). [J. CO2 Util. 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212982018306772)
- **Pore size.** In Fe/zeolite systems there is a **volcano-type relation between zeolite pore size and C5–C11 selectivity**. 10-membered-ring H-ZSM-5 gives the highest liquid-fuel and aromatics selectivity. Zeolite acidity cracks heavier C5+ and lowers the olefin/paraffin ratio. [Appl. Catal. B 2024, S0926337324005253](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926337324005253)
- **1D versus 3D 10-MR.** The CoFe/zeolite paper also studied 10-MR zeolites with 1D and 3D channel systems for CO2 to gasoline (C5–C11). [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354942049_Direct_conversion_of_CO2_to_a_jet_fuel_over_CoFe_alloy_catalysts)
- **Crystal thickness.** Thinner HZSM-5 nanosheets (20 nm) maximise C5+ at isoconversion and reduce coking, a diffusion-length effect. [IECR 2022](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00460)
- **Hydrogen spillover.** Co promotes H spillover from metallic Co onto ZrO2, which facilitates formation and hydrogenation of the key intermediate. [IECR 2022](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00460)
- **Acid tuning by Ga.** Ga substitution in ZSM-5 tunes acidity, raising gasoline selectivity and slowing deactivation. [PubMed 42299751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/)
- **Zn-modified beta.** Combined with In2O3-Co, Zn-modified beta gives predominantly iso-paraffins (snippet; unconfirmed). Large 12-MR pores plus hydrogen transfer favour branched paraffins. [PMC9592991 review (via search summary)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9592991/)
- **Proximity.** Oxide–zeolite interaction depends strongly on spatial proximity. [Carbon Neutralization 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cnl2.70153)
  - Too close: ion exchange poisons acid sites. [PMC10988559](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10988559/)
  - Too far: methanol drain is too slow and RWGS dominates. [Nat. Chem. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.2794)

**Why CH4 and CO form**
- **CO:** formed by RWGS on the oxide. Its higher apparent Ea (117 versus 103 kJ/mol) means CO selectivity rises with temperature. Typical OX-ZEO CO selectivity is above 30%; the Guo GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5 catalyst reaches 11.7%.
  - [PMC10095753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10095753/); [Catal. Today review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920586123002432); [PubMed 42299751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/)
- **CH4:** about 1% or less on In2O3/HZSM-5 and GaZrOx/Ga-ZSM-5. By contrast, the FT-type Fe–Mn–K catalyst gives 10.4% CH4.
  - [Nat. Chem. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.2794); [PubMed 42299751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299751/); [Nat. Commun. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20214-z)

### Inferences
- **Expected product slate from MFI-based OX-ZEO**, consistent with the cited facts but not quantified in any retrieved abstract:
  - mostly C5–C11;
  - iso-paraffins plus methyl-substituted aromatics (dominated by tri- and tetramethylbenzenes in aromatics-selective systems);
  - few olefins, because spilled-over H and hydrogen transfer saturate them;
  - few naphthenes;
  - n-paraffins as a minor fraction.
- **Why MFI caps the carbon number.** The 10-MR channels (about 5.5 Å) let C5–C11 products leave and favour β-scission of heavier carbenium ions, so C12+ alkanes are small. To shift toward C8–C16:
  - use lower acid density, which reduces cracking and hydrogen transfer;
  - use 1D medium-pore zeolites (TON/ZSM-22, MTT/ZSM-23), which favour mono-branched iso-paraffins and suppress aromatics;
  - use 12-MR beta, which allows heavier alkylated aromatics or iso-paraffins;
  - add an olefin oligomerisation stage.

  This is mechanistic reasoning only; I found no OX-ZEO paper quantifying it for C8–C16.
- **Mechanism for modelling.** Treat methanol (with DME in equilibrium) as the single transported intermediate. Treat ketene as at most a minor parallel path for C2. This is consistent with the NMR, DFT and ZnCrOx|SAPO-34 evidence above.

### Gaps
- Not found: an isotope-labelling or operando study that distinguishes ketene from methanol specifically for In2O3/ZSM-5 or ZnZrOx/ZSM-5 in CO2 feeds.
- Not found: quantitative PIONA or carbon-number (Cn) distributions for any OX-ZEO CO2 catalyst; abstracts only give lumps (C5+, aromatics, iso-paraffins).
- Not retrieved: activation energies for H-transfer and β-scission specific to CO2-derived feeds (with water and CO present).

## (B') Published kinetic models: (i) CO2 → methanol on In2O3/ZnZrOx; (ii) RWGS; (iii) MTH on ZSM-5; (iv) combined tandem CO2-to-hydrocarbon models

### Takeaway
Two families of tandem CO2-to-hydrocarbon kinetic models exist:
- **Chalmers (Ghosh, Olsson, Creaser), Chem. Eng. J. 2022:** an **LHHW In2O3 methanol + RWGS model** combined with a **lumped MTH model on HZSM-5**. It reproduces RWGS suppression and the effect of the oxide:zeolite mass ratio.
- **UPV/EHU Bilbao (Portillo, Parra, Aguayo, Ereña, Bilbao, Ateka), ACS Sustain. Chem. Eng. 2024:** an **In2O3–ZrO2/SAPO-34** model with a 12-step network plus **coke deactivation** and CO co-feed.

The In2O3 step is described by:
- the Ghosh 2021 LHHW model (single-site and dual-site variants; fitted Ea of about 35.7 and 54.5 kJ/mol in the single-site version, step assignment unknown);
- DFT microkinetic models (ACS Catal. 2021; J. Catal. 2018).

The MTH step is described by:
- the Bilbao lumped models (Gayubo/Aguayo; 400–550 °C, HZSM-5);
- the Park & Froment carbenium-ion model (726 elementary steps, 225 species);
- Kumar/Svelle/Olsbye SEMK on H-ZSM-5 (Si/Al 200; 21 activation energies plus 8 protonation enthalpies regressed);
- a SEMK on H-ZSM-23 (33 adjustable parameters);
- Standl et al. 2020 (ZSM-5 with olefin co-feed).

**Full parameter tables could not be retrieved** because every full text was blocked.

### Inferences
- **Suggested model architecture** for a Guo-type GaZrOx (or In2O3/ZnZrOx) + ZSM-5 jet-fuel model. This is my synthesis of the cited model families; the specific rate forms below are standard textbook LHHW/lumped forms, **not** copied from the papers:
  1. **Oxide, methanol synthesis:**
     r_MeOH = k1·(P_CO2·P_H2 − P_MeOH·P_H2O/(K_eq1·P_H2²)) / (1 + K_H2^0.5·P_H2^0.5 + K_CO2·P_CO2 + K_H2O·P_H2O)^n
     - Ghosh 2021 structure (single- or dual-site).
     - Use apparent Ea of about 100 kJ/mol as a sanity anchor.
  2. **Oxide, RWGS:**
     r_RWGS = k2·(P_CO2·P_H2 − P_CO·P_H2O/K_eq2) / (same denominator)
     - Ea about 115–120 kJ/mol, so RWGS outruns methanol synthesis at high T.
  3. **Zeolite, methanol/DME equilibrium:** a fast step.
  4. **Zeolite, MTH:** use Bilbao-style lumps (oxygenates → C2–C4 olefins → C5+ → paraffins and aromatics, with coke). For carbon-number resolution, use a SEMK (Kumar 2013; ZSM-23 SEMK) with methylation, oligomerisation, β-scission and hydride-transfer families.
  5. **Hydrogenation term:** an olefin → paraffin step with an H2-dependent rate constant, to represent H spilled over from the oxide.
  6. **Deactivation:** a Bilbao-type activity term, a = exp(−k_d·t), or a coke-concentration-dependent term.
  7. **Water inhibition:** a water term in both denominators.
- **SEMK suits jet-fuel resolution.** The SEMK approach (parameters per reaction family and per carbenium-ion type s/t) is the only literature framework that naturally predicts a full C1–C16 distribution with iso/normal resolution. The ZSM-23 SEMK, with only 2 alkylation/cracking Ea values, is the most compact starting point for 1D 10-MR zeolites.
- **Lumped models will not resolve C8–C16.** The Ghosh 2022 and Portillo 2024 lumped models are fitted to light olefins or gasoline lumps, so they will **not** resolve C8–C16 without re-parametrisation.
